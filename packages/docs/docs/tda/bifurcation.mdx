# Bifurcation Sweep

## Method

We sweep the perturbation amplitude $A$ from 0 (smooth disk) to 0.3 (transitional disk) in steps of 0.05, running 4000 systems at each value. All other parameters are held fixed ($\gamma = 1.0$, $c_\text{migI} = 0$, seed = 42).

All persistence computations use the corrected preprocessing pipeline: log1p transform on skewed features (total terrestrial mass, average terrestrial mass, mass efficiency) followed by standardization. See [Persistent Homology: Preprocessing](persistence#preprocessing) for details on why this is necessary.

This directly addresses Phase D of the project proposal: **does the smooth-to-transitional disk transition produce a topological bifurcation in the planetary architecture space?**

The seven runs are listed in the [run log](../simulations/runs).

## Interpretation

### $H_1$: degeneracy structure across the perturbation sweep

The number of persistent $H_1$ loops varies with $A$:

$$
1 \to 1 \to 0 \to 0 \to 2 \to 1 \to 0
$$

Three **degeneracy-free windows** appear at $A = 0.1$, $A = 0.15$, and $A = 0.3$, where no persistent $H_1$ features survive ($\beta_1 = 0$ at the 0.5 threshold). In these windows, the inverse problem is topologically well-posed: every planetary architecture maps to a connected region of disk initial conditions.

The smooth disk ($A = 0$) has one robust degeneracy loop (lifetime 0.74) that persists into $A = 0.05$ (lifetime 0.50, at the threshold boundary) before disappearing at $A = 0.10$. Degeneracies reappear at $A = 0.20$ (2 loops) and $A = 0.25$ (1 loop), then vanish again at $A = 0.30$.

The non-monotonic behavior at intermediate $A$ values reflects the interplay between the cosine perturbation structure and the characteristic scales of planet formation. As $A$ increases, the radial positions of over-dense and under-dense regions shift relative to the snow line and other critical radii, creating and then resolving different formation pathway degeneracies.

### $H_0$: cluster structure softens

The dominant $H_0$ lifetime generally decreases with $A$:

$$
12.1 \to 8.2 \to 4.5 \to 4.4 \to 4.5 \to 2.5 \to 4.8
$$

The gap between the 1st and 2nd $H_0$ lifetimes drops from 9.6 (smooth) to $\sim$1--2 (transitional). The dominant $H_0$ feature at $A = 0$ is driven by a small number of systems with extreme $N_\text{giant}$ values (see [Persistence: $H_0$](persistence#h_0-formation-channels) for discussion). Despite this outlier sensitivity, the trend is genuine: the density perturbation **softens the cluster structure**, reducing the separation between the most distinct populations as intermediate formation pathways emerge.

### Key finding

The perturbation amplitude $A$ acts as a **bifurcation parameter** for the topology of the planetary architecture space:

1. **Degeneracy resolution**: smooth disks have one irreducible degeneracy ($H_1$ loop) where distinct formation histories produce indistinguishable architectures. The perturbation resolves this at $A = 0.1$ and again at $A = 0.3$, with transient reappearance at intermediate values.

2. **Cluster softening**: the dominant formation channel separation weakens with $A$, indicating that dust traps create intermediate formation pathways that blur the boundaries between distinct populations.

3. **Classification invariance**: the giant planet fraction remains stable at $\sim$31% across all values of $A$. The topological changes are **not driven by shifts in population fractions** but by reorganization of the internal structure of the outcome space. This is precisely the kind of structure that KDE-on-marginals cannot detect.

## Next steps

1. **Finer sampling** around $A = 0.1$ and $A = 0.2$ to sharpen the transition boundaries.
2. **Fiber analysis** at $A = 0$: identify which disk configurations contribute to the $H_1$ loop and verify that $F^{-1}(y)$ is disconnected.
3. **Vary the perturbation length scale** $f$ as a second bifurcation parameter, building a 2D bifurcation diagram in $(A, f)$ space.
